# Active Next.js task configuration

The `web/` app now uses 4 examples (clean2400 ordinals 1092, 973, 1285, 3) with Ours, AdaVec and LIVE: 12 editing attempts per participant, following the existing practice. The fourth example is a local curvature edit of a crescent. Its native outputs are reused from the existing benchmark, with hashes and input identity recorded in `web/study_assets/B0003/provenance.json`.

The proposed recruitment range is 18–24 formal participants, plus separate pilots. This is a resource plan, not a completed power calculation or participant result. Existing timer, randomization, scoring and collection behavior are unchanged by this task-data update. The selected examples are not a random or representative sample.

## Historical designs

The following sections describe the archived static pilot, the earlier formal design, and the old paper study.

## A. Current public pilot

- 3 clean2400 examples: ordinals 1092, 973 and 1285.
- 4 anonymized options: Ours, AdaVec, VTracer and LIVE.
- 12 formal editing cells per session.
- 90-second cap per option.
- Examples were chosen from previously displayed qualitative cases for pilot convenience; they are not random or representative.
- Purpose: interface iteration and limited workflow testing.

## B. Earlier written formal editing design

- 2 methods: Ours and AdaVec.
- 6 examples/tasks.
- 2 practice trials plus 12 formal edits per participant.
- 12 formal participants, plus 2 separate pilot participants.
- Counterbalanced task order and method order.
- 90-second initial cap, to be checked once in the two-person pilot and then frozen.
- Three task families, two examples each: extension, curvature adjustment and notch adjustment.

The source protocol is `formal_6x2_reference/实验设计.md`. It is a planned protocol, not evidence that the participant study was run.

## C. Old paper pairwise study

- Ours plus four baselines.
- 15 participants and 16 pairwise judgments per participant in the historical manuscript.
- Participants judged perceived similarity/editability rather than performing timed edits.

This historical pairwise study must not be used as evidence for the current hands-on editor.
